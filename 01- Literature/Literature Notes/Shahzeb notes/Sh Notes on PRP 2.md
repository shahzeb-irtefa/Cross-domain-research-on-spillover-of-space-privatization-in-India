## Primary research paper 2
## An important assumption
Technological improvement plays an important role in economic growth. (premise)
But the caveat is localized specialized mission oriented investment in R&D rarely diffuses to the general public and only modestly affects economic growth instead of broad general R&D spending which provides a good stimulus for economic growth.
This paper reveals the limits of technological improvements on economic growth (basically telling how much weight the premise holds.)
## Why did this research paper was written?
A lot of countries have spent a lot of money on R&D with the aim to drive local and overall economic growth both in the short term ans long term with the justification of the massive spending US government did post Sputnik on NASA, famously known as the moonshot. This research's goal was to find how much does public R&D affect short and long term economic growth both locally and nationally.

> $158 Billion spent on R&D by OECD governments

## The background of this paper:

The researchers are asking does R&D funding by the government actually have a positive impact on economy given the private institutions underinvest in R&D even though the technological breakthroughs help both privately and the public. How much of an effect does public R&D have and does the gap bridged by the government actually have an effect?

The goal of the paper is to check whether public R&D expenditure has long term economic growth both locally and in the broader economy

The researchers developed a new empirical way to isolate the spillover of economic growth in neighbouring industry (Relevant to our research, can give us a glimpse of how to measure economic spill over in neighbouring industries)

## Why is the government investing on R&D?

Private institutions underinvest in R&D because a lot of benefits are public and not private from which they can profit off from. The government spends to fill this gap created by underinvesting in order to keep technological progress moving forward. 

## The endogeneity problem:
Endogeneity roughly means being born within the system, here it is asking whether there is a third factor that is producing the outcome or is it just the factors within consideration (Here by third factor I mean a factor which hypothetically is driving both X and Y thus creating an illusion that X is causing Y to grow or vice versa), or is there a case of reverse causality
For example: There are lot of cafes near universities but this does not mean universities are present due large number of cafes. (This is an example of reverse causality.)

### How this problem appears in this research paper
NASA cherry picked the best of the best in order to increase the chances of success, the question is did NASA's spending caused their growth or did NASA just picked the one already destined to succeed.
Basically here we can't identify whether public R&D investment improved technological capabilities. 

### The solution:
They used CIA's declassified information to map out technological baseline of US untainted by the region's different factor. This helped them identify the hard baseline from where the US started.

> **Technical terminology:**
> Triple difference statistical model, basically this means they had three different dimensions and they used the interaction between these dimensions and mix and matched them, took their differences to reduce the possibility of a fourth hidden factor driving all the growth.

> Technology harvesting highlighted the high exposure/high capability areas and DDD method measured whether the investment actually resulted in overall growth and TFP (total factor production) increase.


## How did they define neighbouring industries?
They used CIA's declassified files to find the level of technology soviet union had post sputnik (this would be the goal that NASA had to surpass/ but first reach there.).
Then they compared these technologies to relevant US patents before 1958 and compared the demand of these technologies post 1958. Industries where demand increased were classified as sensitive neighbouring industries.

# Module 2 : Using Gen AI and Agentic AI in literature review

# Generative and Agentic AI

Generative AI: They give direct responses by searching and finding patterns through sifting from large data sets.
It is like asking a knowledgeable person, they give their response directly.
Consumer grade generative AI has issues that limits its use for research, they are mainly lack of traceability and hallucination.

Modern AI hallucinates a lot and is confident in every answer, what we want is that the AI should feel doubt and answer that it doesn't know, pose questions,etc. but these go against the reward systems used to train LLMs. 

We need traceability and transparency.

Agentic AI on the other hand is more like asking someone to go through multiple sources, analyze differences,etc.

The main difference is generative AI gives a direct response in a single step where as agentic AI is multi step and gives through collecting and going through multiple sources.

# Traditional vs AI search

Traditional search/ Boolean searching, it is deterministic meaning it gives results that exactly match the keywords and will mostly produce the same result no matter how many times you search.

AI search on the other hand is much more probabilistic, it answers based on the underlying concept

## Why AI search is powerful?

When researching often times we don't know the exact terminology and in cross domain research this could be a problem, AI often sifts through the material and responds with answers that are in the general ball park of what we asked so it is much more flexible and will help in finding out more about a topic and give us results that would have been missed by the rigid traditional boolean searching

# Module 4: How to enhance your chances of serendipitous research discovery.

Serendipity in research means finding something valuable by accident and pursuing it leading to a much more valuable and great outcome.

1. How to prepare your mind for serendipity?
   . Serendipity is a skill and can be honed
   . Become comfortable with plans going awry; let go of rigid plans; be open to and try to come up with different perspectives.
   . Take on characteristics of a 'super-encounter' and appreciate the importance of information; believe you are one; make head space for incubation of ideas; look for connections around you

   Increase the number of participants and diversity of those, increasing the number of diverse minds will help in finding new perspectives.

3. Embrace failure.
   . History suggests we should make room for errors
   . Consider loosening the controls on experiment design

# Module 4: How to identify research gaps.

Research gap is an area or topic for which missing or inadequate information limits the ability of reviewers to reach a conclusive answer for a specific question.

1. Find for a gap in research papers (most common)
   . What is the significance of this research to my work or the broader field?
   . Does the author's argument require more clarification?
   . What issues or questions has the author not addressed?
   . Is there a different perspective that I can consider?
   . Are the methods or procedures used outdated or no longer considered valid in my field? Is there scope for me to test the findings using a more current approach?

2. Find keywords and terms related to specific keywords (used for filtration)
3. Seek help from seniors and discuss
4. Use digital tools to seek out popular topics or most cited research papers
5. Check websites of popular journals 
   . The websites of prominent journals often have a section called 'key concepts' where experts in an area highlight the central ideas in that field.
6. Note down your question and make sure to cite where the question arose from.
7. Research on each question (meaning has this question been asked previously or has it been answered)
8. Beware of literature gap (A literature gap is often preceded by a research gap).
   **Expressions that often indicate gaps:**
   **... has/have not been ... (studied/reported/elucidated)**
   **... is required/needed ...**
   **... the key question is/remains ...**
   **... it is important to address ...**
   **... future areas of research ...**

9. Read systematic reviews (Can be found on google scholar)
